Concept note for Nick and Nick — please read this one properly, because it is the item with the most moving parts.

Every figure on this screen is made up. It is a worked example so you can see the shape of the thing, not Guest Guardian data. The owner, the address, the payouts and the reviews are all invented for the demo.

What is genuinely proven here. The layout, the statements, the itemised breakdown and the calendar are all real working screens. And the "Add to home screen" button up the top is not a mock-up either. Open this page on your phone, add it, and you get a Guest Guardian icon on your home screen that opens straight into this dashboard with no browser bar around it. That is the app-like experience you asked about, and it is the same approach we already run for our own clients.

What still has to be confirmed before we could promise the real thing. Your booking engine comes from Holiday Future, who resell Hostaway. To pull your actual statements in here we need them to give you API access, which is a technical door they either open or they do not. We have not asked them yet, and we are not going to tell you it will definitely be there. There are two honest routes:

Route one, the quick one. Hostaway already has its own owner portal. If Holiday Future will let it sit on your own web address, owners go to owners.guestguardian.com.au and never see the words "holiday future" anywhere. That removes the screenshotting immediately and is mostly a phone call.

Route two, the proper one. With API access we build what you are looking at now and feed it your live data, so the reporting is in your brand end to end and can show things their portal does not.

Either way, the screenshotting stops. The question is only how much of it ends up in your brand, and that is a question for Holiday Future rather than for us. It is worth asking them early, because the answer changes what this costs to build.

An owner only ever sees the properties ticked against their name. They cannot see each other, and they cannot see anything about your business.

The short answer

You add it in Hostaway. It appears here.

You should never type a property into two systems. Whichever way this ends up working, that rule does not change.

Preferred

If we can connect to Hostaway

  1. You add the property in Hostaway, exactly as you do today.
  2. It appears on this screen on its own, usually within minutes.
  3. You tick "show on the website", pick a suburb page and write the description.
  4. Its own page goes live, with live availability and live pricing.

Nothing is typed twice. Photos, bedrooms, prices and the calendar all stay in Hostaway, which stays the single source of truth for anything a guest could book.

Fallback

If we cannot

  1. You add the property in Hostaway as usual.
  2. You add it here once too: name, photos, sleeps, bedrooms.
  3. The booking button still points at your real booking engine.

Still perfectly workable, and it is how the site runs right now. The cost is that a property you rename or re-photograph has to be updated in both places. At four listings that is a minor annoyance. At forty it is a job.

And the one thing we would not do, even though it sounds appealing.

We would not take the bookings ourselves and retire your current engine. Your homes are on Airbnb and Stayz as well as direct, and something has to make a night vanish from all of them the instant it sells anywhere. That job is the entire reason your booking platform exists, and if we took it on and our sync ran a few minutes late, the same night sells twice and two families arrive at one house on a Friday evening.

So the split we would recommend is this. Anything a guest can book stays with the platform that already does it. Everything else, which is how it looks, what it says, who it reaches and what your owners can see, is ours. You already have the guest side working. It just currently looks like somebody else's.
