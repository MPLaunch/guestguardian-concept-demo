Direct bookingsWhere the payment happens

Three versions of the same booking. The difference is what the guest sees in the address bar at the moment they hand over a card, and what each version costs to get there.

Today

The guest is handed over

They see: guestguardian.holidayfuture.com

The site does its job right up to "book", then the guest lands on a different company's address, in a different design, and pays there.

Cost to change: nothing, it is what happens now.

Cheapest fix

Your address, their checkout

They see: book.guestguardian.com.au

Hostaway lets the booking engine live on your own domain. The checkout still looks like theirs, but the word holidayfuture disappears from the journey completely.

What it takes: a setting inside your Hostaway account and one DNS record. No software, no new way for anything to break.

Worth knowing: every one of your four listings already carries an empty booking-engine address field, so the slot is sitting there unused.

Built, and live on this demo

Your page, the whole way

They see: guestguardian.com.au

Dates, price, guest details and the card step all on your own page, in your own brand. This is what the property pages do now — pick some dates and follow it through.

What it takes: a server holding your Hostaway key, the payment linked to the Stripe account already connected to Hostaway, and the reservation written back into Hostaway.

Not free. This one is a software build, not a setting.

The one thing that does not change in any of the three.

Hostaway keeps the calendar. It is what makes a night vanish from Airbnb and Stayz the instant it sells anywhere, and that job stays exactly where it is. Even in the third version, the booking is written straight back into Hostaway and blocks the channels the same as it does today. The card simply gets typed somewhere that says Guest Guardian.

And the honest part. The third version has to rebuild everything your current checkout already does for free: the length-of-stay discount, the cleaning fee, extra guests, the optional extras, coupon codes and your cancellation terms. This demo reproduces all of them from your real Hostaway settings, and prices an eight night stay at The Bungalow to the same cent your own checkout does. It still has to be paid for once, properly, rather than assumed.
Who actually receives the money today.

Worth separating, because the address bar makes it look like somebody else is taking it. Airbnb genuinely is a middleman: it collects from the guest, keeps its cut and pays you later. Your booking engine is not that. It is a card machine on your own counter, and there is a Stripe account connected to your Hostaway that the money runs through. The one thing nobody has confirmed is whose Stripe account that is, and that is the answer that decides who the merchant is on a direct booking. It is a two minute check inside Stripe.
